# Category Theory

I came across a [playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLbgaMIhjbmEnaH_LTkxLI7FMa2HsnawM_) about **Category Theory**. In it, **Bartosz Milewski**, a PhD in Quantum Physics and author of *Category Theory for Programmers*, gives an excellent lecture on one of the great concepts of mathematical abstraction and how it can give us a better view of the world. After all, mathematics is nothing more than a technique invented by *Homo sapiens* to describe how things work (or not, as quantum physics might say) in the world.

[![Category Theory](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5b1d427ae0c922e912eda447/5b567a0c7e0e9823d4098013_letrois.jpg "A small example")](https://www.math3ma.com/blog/what-is-category-theory-anyway)

## A world of functions and types

Abstraction goes a long way in category theory. Unlike [set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_theory), we do not deal with elements and all their nuances here; the abstraction goes further. In his videos, Milewski often uses set theory to make a connection with the abstraction found in category theory. **Composition** (*composability*) and **identity** are fundamental concepts in category theory.

Many of these ideas seem abstract and strange if you are not familiar with [functional programming](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programa%C3%A7%C3%A3o_funcional). If you are, a lot of what gets done starts to make sense. I recommend it! 👍
