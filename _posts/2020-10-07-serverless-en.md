I always like trying to predict the future, even though getting it right is hard. I find it fun to look at the past and present and see which path the world seems to be taking.

The first programming language I fell in love with was C#. I cannot say why. I had already worked with [VB.NET](http://VB.NET) and **Java** at the time, but Microsoft's ecosystem and Visual Studio 2010 gave me some comfort in the unfamiliar world of programming.

A lot has changed, and I have changed as a professional too. I kept learning new things, and overnight a whole new world of front-end development appeared that I did not know. **Angular**, **React**, **Vue**: *choose your player*. I was too used to **Bootstrap** and **jQuery**, and it took me a while to understand why this change happened and all the advantages it brought.

## **FaaS**

If there was a revolution on the front end, on the back end we saw the rise of **RESTful** APIs and the **MVC** pattern, with some apps using **BFF**. All of that was fine, but what I found most interesting was **serverless**.

With the massive move to the **cloud**, we left local servers behind. With **Docker**, we left virtual machines behind. With the creation of **Lambda functions**, perhaps we will leave APIs as we know them behind.

## **AWS as a back end**

Amazon dominates the cloud market today. It moved first and is now reaping the rewards. One very interesting thing to look at is the **Free Tier**, meaning products that are free up to a certain limit.

For example: **DynamoDB** up to 25 GB. **AWS Lambda** up to 1 million free executions per month. **AWS Cognito** up to 50,000 users.

That made me think that it is now possible to **build a complete application at no cost**.

## **Gatsby as a front end**

Gatsby is one of these static-site generators, like **Jekyll**, **Hugo**, and so on. It is written entirely in JavaScript, natively supports **React**, and uses **GraphQL**. Today, it is possible to host static sites at no cost, for example on **Netlify**.

## **Conclusion**

We really are living in an age of abundance. There are more movies, games, and songs than we could consume in our entire lives, for the price of a monthly subscription or even at no cost through other means.

As the cost of running a data center falls, that abundance is already becoming tangible. **Today, it is possible to build a system with zero maintenance cost, 24/7 availability, and redundant data.**

It is amazing. ✨

Where could this lead? I believe the cost of some services will drop significantly, while a huge space opens up for free and open-source systems, or *pay-as-you-go* services.

**The only cost that remains is the labor** required to create and maintain these systems. Information is abundant and free, but knowledge has never been more valuable than it is today.
